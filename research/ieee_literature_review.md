# SmartBus literature review and source-verification report

**Prepared:** 29 October 2026
**Project reviewed:** SmartBus, repository snapshot `f885ae6` (`main`)
**Purpose:** Verify the six supplied IEEE references, compare their reported work with the current application code, and provide a cautious Related Work draft.

**Scope update:** This was the initial tracking/attendance review. The project now has a capacity-aware breakdown-recovery prototype; its current research direction and implementation limits are in [`proposed_contribution.md`](proposed_contribution.md). The Related Work draft below is retained as a literature baseline and should be revised for the final paper's selected research question.

## Scope and verification limits

IEEE Xplore records were used to confirm titles, DOI strings, venue, year, and available abstracts. Full text could not be consistently retrieved from IEEE Xplore in this review, so the methods/evaluation/limitations below are restricted to what the indexed publisher abstract reports. Where the Xplore record did not expose author or pagination fields, the bibliographic details were cross-checked against proceedings metadata or academic indexing records. Those secondary checks are noted in the source table. Verify all final reference formatting against the downloadable IEEE citation export before submission.

The project comparison is a static code inspection, not a live deployment review or a test run. In particular, an API route existing on the server does not establish that the Flutter interface calls it.

## Application evidence used for the comparison

The README describes four role portals and lists tracking, QR attendance, alerts and management features. The inspected implementation qualifies those claims:

- [`lib/screens/incharge/incharge_qr_scanner.dart`](../lib/screens/incharge/incharge_qr_scanner.dart) shows a camera-like placeholder and an explicit **Simulate Scan** button. That button calls `SmartBusProvider.addAttendanceRecord`; manual attendance does the same. No camera/QR scanning dependency is listed in [`pubspec.yaml`](../pubspec.yaml).
- [`lib/screens/location_scanner_screen.dart`](../lib/screens/location_scanner_screen.dart) offers **Simulate secure QR scan**. `SmartBusProvider` holds route/location data in memory and contains a local breakdown reroute simulation. It is not evidence of live GPS collection or a real QR validation.
- [`lib/providers/smart_bus_provider.dart`](../lib/providers/smart_bus_provider.dart) initializes routes from `MockData` and modifies the local route list during rerouting.
- The backend does include PostgreSQL persistence and REST operations for attendance/fleet/emergencies. However, the in-charge screen inspected above does not call the attendance endpoint. [`backend/src/main/java/com/smartbus/tracking/controller/TrackingController.java`](../backend/src/main/java/com/smartbus/tracking/controller/TrackingController.java) broadcasts incoming WebSocket updates; this alone does not establish a working authenticated driver-location client.
- Therefore, compare these publications to SmartBus as a prototype with a partially built backend and simulated client workflows. Do not write that SmartBus has deployed/validated live tracking, camera QR capture, GPS-verified attendance, or measured ETA performance based only on the present code.

## Draft Related Work

College transportation applications have used mobile devices and cloud services to provide campus-specific tracking and management. Yeruva *et al.* describe EduBusGuru, which combines driver-smartphone GPS, Flutter, Firebase synchronization, and role-specific tools for administrators, students, and drivers [1]. Its scope is the closest match to SmartBus, so the presence of a mobile app, route administration, and location tracking by itself is not a defensible novelty claim. Suresh *et al.* extend the tracking problem toward arrival-time estimation by describing GPS/IoT tracking and traffic- and weather-informed ETA calculation [2]. de la Rocha and Wangham instead examine mobile crowdsensing as a lower-cost alternative intended to address GPS/network availability and collaborative-sensing security; their abstract reports prototype evaluation in functionality, energy consumption, and performance [3]. These works motivate evaluation of location freshness, ETA error, coverage during connectivity loss, device energy use, and location-data integrity. Their abstracts do not provide enough quantitative detail to compare reported performance directly.

QR-based attendance research similarly shows that scanning a code is only one part of a trustworthy attendance workflow. Liew and Tan report checking the class hour, registered device, and geolocation before recording attendance [4]. Pathan *et al.* describe randomized/dynamic QR codes combined with real-time location checking [5]. Bacuna and Dadiz describe a server-backed QR workflow that encodes session fields and produces attendance summaries, while also collecting student facial photos [6]. Together, these studies suggest that identity, time, place, replay resistance, and auditable server-side records should be treated as separate design and evaluation concerns. Their classroom settings do not directly establish that the same checks are appropriate or usable for bus boarding.

SmartBus brings together student, in-charge, admin, HOD, and manager portals, and its backend contains database-backed attendance and operations APIs. Code inspection nevertheless finds important gaps between the user-facing representation and an operational system: the in-charge QR screen presents a simulated scan, the location sharing entry point is also simulated, and its local route/rerouting state is seeded from mock data. The implementation examined here therefore does not yet demonstrate camera-based QR capture, bus-location updates from a verified driver device, location-checked boarding, or validated arrival estimates. A later scope review identified emergency recovery as a more direct research direction because the app already contains a breakdown workflow. This baseline literature also shows that spare-capacity planning and adaptive bus operations during disruptions are established topics [7], [8]. The current SmartBus prototype should therefore be evaluated as a campus-scale, transparent dispatch workflow, not presented as a new general bus-bridging optimization method. See [`proposed_contribution.md`](proposed_contribution.md) for the revised research question and evaluation design.
